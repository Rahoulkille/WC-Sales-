01 · Identity

What we are: two phases, one destination Direction agreed 6 Aug · trigger numbers pending

Owner: Andrew (external narrative) / whole group (substance) · Source: strategy session 6 Aug 2026, refined 10 Aug

We are running two strategies in sequence, deliberately, with a defined gate between them. They are distinct phases, not a blend - and the boundary between them is the most important management object in this document.

Phase 1 · now · The Outcomes Play

Turbo Pickle is the performance layer for casino marketing. We know casino games better than anyone, and we sell what that knowledge does: creative that carries what a game feels like, every game dressed for the lobby, campaigns measured where it counts. Narrow and high; margin proves fast and funds itself. The engine never leaves the building - except as shipped product, covered below. This is not a waiting room. It has active clients (FanDuel, Hard Rock Digital, Flutter brands, Evolution, Betway, Hollywoodbets), it fits the vision, and it is a complete, viable business that will still exist inside Phase 2 as the top layer.

Phase 2 · on trigger · The Two-Layer Play

Turbo Pickle becomes the content and intelligence infrastructure for online casino. A base layer of live game data and media - wide, cheap, zero-touch - sold behind an engineered airlock; a top layer of served intelligence and applied outcomes where margin, differentiation and case studies live. The wide-and-cheap intelligence play survives inside this phase as the base rungs of the ladder, kept commercially viable by watermarking, hashed embeddings, rate limits and licence terms with teeth. Deep intelligence - the tuned weights, the raw embedding space, the engines - is never for sale, in any phase.

The transition trigger

A compound gate. All four conditions required - any three without the fourth is not the trigger. Each condition is a tracked number (section 09), so the transition happens on evidence, not on mood or opportunity.

Why a hard phase boundary and not a blend: two reasons from the strategy debate, both still true. First, the exposure fork is the one irreversible decision in the business - selling shallow data wide cannot be undone, so it should be walked through deliberately, from strength, with T4 done. Second, running both revenue ramps at once demands two full motions from a company our size; T3 is what makes the second motion affordable. Phasing is how we avoid being pigeonholed as only-ever-B without paying C's costs before we can carry them.

Ready products ship in Phase 1. Where a base-layer product is refined, shipped and protected, we sell it now - the metadata feed is the first, sanctioned and live in the SkyCity proposal. This is not scope creep and not a breach of the phase rule; it is the Phase 2 muscle being built one finished product at a time. The rule is the readiness, not the calendar: unfinished base-layer products do not get sold early because a deal is tempting.

Vision, naming, tone

"To transform how casino games find their audience" - true in both phases; the audience is the point, the intelligence is the means. It is all Turbo Pickle; Wildcards will be sunset, with staff comms in the company update inside two weeks, framed as an identity shift. Tone of voice is agreed: informal, direct, no jargon, knowledgeable. "Game DNA" remains the one piece of vocabulary we allow ourselves.

02 · The moat

The engine Draft for validation

Owner: Joe (build) · Source: SkyCity deck v6, Aug 2026

One engine sits behind everything, in both phases. We play every game automatically on release, record and measure it, and extract the detail from the game itself rather than the supplier's marketing pack. The output is one structured record per game - the Game DNA - which every product line reads from. Completing it is priority one: 100% of the active catalogue observed, embedded and enriched. In Phase 1 the record powers outcomes; in Phase 2 it is also the base-layer product. Same engine, wider door.

The pipeline, plainly: game announced → played automatically on release → record built (every field, art and video captured) → assets generated in the client's design system → humans check specs, brand and compliance → live on release day. The manual industry equivalent is three to five days per game; ours is same day, every game. The margin engine underneath is human-exception operation: every workflow automated to exceptions-only. That same discipline, applied to client onboarding and delivery, is trigger condition T3.

Proof points we already put in front of clients

  • ~40% of a game's launches come through the homepage trailer placement; 62% of those players launch another game from it (live operator deployment).
  • Explore Shorts: +12% eGGR per user A/B tested against control, +6% games played per user, ~$1k modelled GGR per user in full-screen format.
  • Live with FanDuel, Hard Rock Digital, Flutter brands, Evolution, Betway and Hollywoodbets. 32 staff, 80% engineering.

Alignment check for the group

  • Coverage number. "45,000+ games" is the ingested total. What percentage of the active regulated-market catalogue is that? This single number is the gap between the infrastructure claim and reality; it should be on a weekly dashboard, not estimated in meetings.
  • Field accuracy. We say the record is "checked against what the supplier declared." Do we have an error/discrepancy rate we can quote? A diligence process will ask.
  • Cost per game observed. FILL: £/game - the unit economics of the future base layer start here.

Owner: Rahoul (system) · Andrew (narrative) · James (HubSpot/data)

Two entry tracks, split by account temperature, converging on the same ladder - and dual-track is live now, in Phase 1: cold accounts through automated outbound (Origami), warm accounts through the founder network and trade shows. Two tracks is also the maximum - anything that does not fit the ladder does not ship. Outbound runs on Origami (discovery, enrichment, sequencing; sends from connected mailboxes; CRM integration read-only) with HubSpot free tier for dedup and post-engagement deal tracking. The two-phase search architecture is designed and calibrating on FanDuel as the test account: Phase 1 is broad recall with no seniority filtering (so vanity titles do not silently drop good contacts), Phase 2 classifies into the canonical cohorts. Three canonical repo files govern it: CLAUDE.md, phase2-prompt.md, targeting-prompt.md. Opener copy and sequence content are deliberately deferred until the systems build stabilises and positioning is locked. Tier B cold volume runs from a dedicated sending domain, separate from the main domain.

Launch sequencing (Turbo Pickle)

Launch phases (distinct from the business phases above): gating → teaser → soft launch (coordinated LinkedIn announcement, simple site with HubSpot-integrated signup, conference anchors G2E and SBC Lisbon) → full commercial launch. The gating item - product strategy direction - cleared on 6 Aug. The next gate is positioning narrative sign-off: no downstream copy, one-pagers or sales assets before it. Andrew's website draft is the venue for that argument.

The say/do gap, handled on purpose

In Phase 1 we sell outcomes while telling an engine story. That gap is a feature if we manage it and a credibility problem if we don't. The rule for all external material: products sell, the engine shows. The "how we do it" section carries the infrastructure narrative as demonstration - coverage counters, the record, Game DNA - without claiming to be infrastructure before the base layer is on sale. 07 · Discipline

What we say no to Agreed in Strategy C

Owner: whole group - this section only works if everyone enforces it on everyone

No selling people's time. Services are a gated exception: strategic logo, staffed off the roadmap, premium priced. Hard Rock is grandfathered as the funding engine - named, bounded, not a precedent.

No deep intelligence out the door - phase-split. In Phase 1, nothing leaves the estate except refined, shipped, protected products (the metadata feed is the first and currently only sanctioned example). At the Phase 2 gate, the tiered rules activate: outputs and shallow data yes, tools and deep data no; served intelligence returns answers through an API, never vectors, never weights; anything wide carries the airlock - watermarking, hashed embeddings, rate limits, licence teeth. The per-product-line exposure table (section 03) is the working detail of this rule and is still unwritten - it is part of T4.

No third motion. Two entry tracks are the maximum. Anything that does not fit the ladder does not ship - however good the opportunistic revenue looks. This is the rule that protects us from ourselves: Strategy C's own paper says it breaks exactly where we are weakest, fragmentation.

08 · Honest mirror

SWOT Draft for validation

Owner: Rahoul (draft) · whole group (truth) · Rule: every item must be falsifiable or quantified; no marketing allowed in here

Strengths

  • The observed record. 45,000+ games played, 555+ fields from play not paraphrase. Nobody researched in section 06 has this; most cannot get it without rebuilding our capture engine.
  • Casino-native, engineering-heavy. 32 staff, 80% engineering, live with FanDuel, Hard Rock Digital, Flutter brands, Evolution, Betway, Hollywoodbets - logos most seed-stage competitors would kill for.
  • Proof with numbers. +12% eGGR A/B tested, ~40% of launches via trailer placement, 62% cross-launch. We sell measured outcomes, not creative opinions.
  • Bootstrapped economics. Hard Rock funds the build; no investor clock forcing a premature scale decision.
  • One engine, many products. Every product line reads from the same record, so each new line is marginal cost, not a new company.

Weaknesses

  • Revenue concentration. Hard Rock is the funding engine. Concentration: FILL: % of revenue. Until that number is on a dashboard and falling, it is our biggest diligence red flag.
  • Perceived as a creative agency. The group itself named this on 6 Aug. The rebrand exists to fix it; until it lands, we get benchmarked against agency day rates.
  • Packaging is unclear. The ladder is unnamed, unpriced, not self-servable. Strategy C's stated failure mode - blurred layers - describes our present state, not a future risk.
  • Twenty-ish people, two live tracks plus a platform build. Warm founder-led selling + cold automated outbound + delivery + Phase 2 readiness work, simultaneously. T3 automation is the designed answer, but until it ships, something is being starved - we should choose what, on purpose.
  • The infrastructure claim outruns reality. Coverage of the active catalogue is FILL: %; the gap is exactly what priority one closes.

Opportunities

  • Category creation. "Content and intelligence infrastructure for online casino" has no incumbent owner. First credible claimant sets the vocabulary buyers use.
  • Optimove/CRM distribution. Game DNA as the game-level feed inside the CRM stack every operator already runs - partner-shaped revenue on the Served rung.
  • Supplier side, later. Suppliers switched on from strength: the same record sold upstream (launch packs, distribution intelligence) roughly doubles the TAM without a new engine.
  • Mobile gaming adjacency. Sequenced after the iGaming beachhead converts. Sett's $57M validates the category; our observed-play approach ports.
  • Regulatory tailwind. Compliance-grade, per-market creative and responsible-gaming metadata get more valuable as regulation tightens - and are hardest for generic platforms to fake.

Threats

  • Build-vs-buy at Tier A. Big groups with in-house studios plus a generic tool (Bannerflow/CHILI/Smartly) is the deal we lose without ever seeing a competitor's name.
  • Bundling from adjacent platforms. Optimove creative generation, VAIX/Sportradar data products, or casino platforms shipping "good enough" tiles/metadata natively.
  • Well-funded entrants. Sett ($57M) or similar pivoting into iGaming with agentic creative; Lobby Magic maturing upward from tiles.
  • Feed leakage. If the Feed scales before the airlock exists, our cheapest product becomes our competitors' free data source. The phased model gates this by design - T4 before wide sale - so the residual threat is discipline: a tempting deal arriving before the protection stack does.
  • Supplier IP pushback. Our record is built by playing suppliers' games and keeping what we find. One aggressive supplier legal letter is a scenario we should have a written position on before launch. Status: Unknown
  • Discipline failure. Named by Strategy C itself: the two layers blur, the Feed gets oversold upward, outcomes get discounted downward, and we recreate the unclear packaging this strategy exists to kill.

10 · Endgame

Exit thesis Draft for validation

Owner: founders · Frame: exit at a SaaS/infrastructure multiple shapes every choice above, from HubSpot timestamps to the ladder

The two-layer play keeps the widest acquirer set open - infrastructure players, suppliers, operators, affiliates, PE - because the mix contains something for each. The multiple is decided by the revenue mix at exit: the more the base layer and served intelligence dominate, the closer we trade to an infrastructure multiple; the more services dominate, the closer to an agency multiple. That is the entire reason the north star is product revenue share and not revenue.
