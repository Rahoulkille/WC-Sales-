How we talk about Turbo Pickle

The sales language layer. Everything a deck, a demo, a website or a cold email says should trace back to a line on this page. Battle-tested lines are marked with where they've worked.

1 · The headline story

Your suppliers gave you games. Not content.

The opener. Used in three decks (bet365, cardcrush, SkyCity). It names the problem every operator recognises.

All the content your casino needs. On tap.

The promise. Playables, ad creative, video, tiles, banners, SEO pages and game data, in your brand, in minutes.

We play every game, so everything we make matches reality.

The reason to believe. The machine plays, records and measures every game as it's released. Everything else is built from that.

Problem, promise, proof. Every pitch is those three sentences in that order, then detail. "Everything an online casino needs to know about its games. Built once." stays available as the data-led variant when talking to CMS, CRM or SEO buyers.

2 · The entry story: playables first

Playables are the front door to Turbo Pickle. Not because they're the most bespoke thing we make, but because they're the clearest demonstration of the model: the making is already done, you just add your brand.

The top games, already playable. Add your brand, hit download.

The library grows every week with the games players are actually searching for. Upload your brand kit once: logo, colours, legal lines. Every playable comes out branded, network-ready, in minutes.

The value words: speed, cost, ease

  • Speed. The top titles each week are in the platform when they launch, not weeks after your brief lands in someone's queue.
  • Cost. You're not paying a build fee per playable. The build happened once; access covers the library.
  • Ease. Brand kit in, playables out. No brief, no rounds of amends, no production calendar.
We don't say

"We build custom playables to your brief." That's the old, slow, expensive story, and it reopens the per-asset conversation.

We do say

"The library covers the games that matter. If a title you need isn't there yet, tell us, that's how the library grows."

5 · Pricing language

The frame: access, not assets. No published rate card. Three cost classes carry every conversation:

  • One-off: getting you started. Backlog packs, design systems, kick-off work.
  • Recurring: keeping you current. Every new title, handled as it releases.
  • Licence: access to what's already built. The library, the data, the feed. This class grows as automation lands, and it's where pricing decouples from asset count.

When someone asks "what does it cost?"

Catalogue size, launch cadence and product priorities are what we price from, so your answers shape the numbers.

Held with SkyCity, accepted without friction. This is the standard first response.

When someone anchors on unit count

Reframe from asset to output, the GOAT move: "That's not 3 videos, that's 3 templates fed with 20+ titles each, 120+ versions." The unit of value is what comes out of the machine, not what goes into the invoice.

When someone resists a minimum commitment

Never justify a floor with our capacity planning, that's the agency talking. Under access, commitment buys price certainty and freshness: "the subscription means every new game is covered the day it lands, and your rate is fixed for the term."

6 · Objection answers

"Is this real software, or people behind a curtain?"

"It's software, and it's more than software." Behind the login is the biggest thing anyone has built around casino games: the machine that plays every game, the data that describes them, the AI and the creative systems that build from it, and the people who trained it all. Whether a given output came from code or a person is the least interesting fact about it. What matters is that you have access to all of it, and the result, the turnaround and the price don't change either way.

For the inverse fear (too automated): "No black box without a door." You approve the templates, every output is reviewable, nothing auto-publishes unless you say so.

"What happens to my in-house team?"

"Automate the floor. Put humans on the ceiling." Your designers focus on hero campaigns and brand work; the production line, every tile, every size, every game, is what we take off them. We're not asking anyone to fire anyone. The platform makes their team faster, which is the whole point of stopping selling against it.

"We'll manage metadata ourselves."

"The operators who said that loudest are often the ones who realised six months in the work hadn't been done. Keep your team on the top 100 titles you actually care about; we handle the long tail, which is most of the catalogue."

"Integration will be a nightmare."

"We can show value before we touch your stack. Your data stays yours; we prove the lift with you before you commit a penny or an engineer." JSON, CSV or XML, their call.

"You're too small."

Don't apologise for size. Lead with the roster, frame size as agility: the reason a game's assets exist in 48 hours is that there's no agency org chart between the machine and the output.
